# The name

## What a frit is

In glassmaking and ceramics, a [frit][frit] is *"a ceramic composition
that has been fused, quenched, and granulated"*. Raw materials are
heated until they combine, dropped into water so the thermal shock
shatters them, then ground to a powder.

The point is that the work happens **before** the real melt. Fritting
renders soluble or toxic components inert, lets gases escape at an
intermediate stage so the finished glass is free of bubbles, and — in
the article's words — grinding the result *"expedites the second part of
the process, which is to reduce the system to glass"*.

## Why the tool has that name

frit indexes work that already exists: plans written, staged on
branches, most of them not yet melted into anything. The index is
prepared material. It does no work itself, and it makes the work that
follows cheaper — which is what a frit is for.

The read-only posture falls out of the same image. Frit sits in the
batch. It is not the furnace.

## Why it is a sibling of atlas

The name is not merely craft-adjacent to [atlas][atlas], the sibling
project. Atlas is *made by fritting*.

Cobalt glass is *"also known as 'atlas' when ground as a pigment"*:
cobalt oxide is smelted with quartz and potassium carbonate, the melt is
quenched in water to shatter it, and the pieces are ground to a coarse
blue powder. Fused, quenched, granulated — the same three steps, on the
same bench. The two names come out of one process rather than one
vocabulary.

## Why not something more obvious

Every evocative word for *watching a fleet* has been claimed by an agent
orchestrator in the last two years. Checked and rejected for collision:
[muster][muster] (MCP control plane), [weft][weft] (AI coding CLI),
[orrery][orrery] (*"workflow planning and orchestration CLI for AI
agents"*), [bosun][bosun] (tmux-native agent orchestrator), and four
separate projects named *babysitter*.

Glassmaking was unclaimed because nobody in this space was looking
there, and because the vocabulary was already ours.
